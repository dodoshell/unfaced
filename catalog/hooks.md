# Hooks

The opening line: the first one to three seconds, which decide whether the viewer stays. A script's `## Hook` section is one hook, and its front matter names the hook type (`hook: 4`). The first frame of the video already shows the hook (the platforms use it as the preview), so the line must work as text on screen as well as spoken.

Rules for every hook:

- Under 12 words, or under 3 seconds spoken. Cut every word that doesn't create a question in the viewer's head.
- Promise something the video really delivers. A hook the body doesn't pay off loses the viewer and the platform's trust.
- No greetings, no "in this video", no channel name.
- Write it for the ear: say it out loud before keeping it.

Each hook below has three examples from different niches. `examples/hooks/` has the same examples; `unfaced reel hooks` renders them as a hook reel, which is also attached to each release.

## 01 Shocking question

**Status:** ready

Open with a question the viewer can't answer but suddenly wants to.

- Write it: a "why" or "what happens" question about something familiar, with one surprising detail in it.
- Examples:
  - Animals: "Why do cats purr when they're hurt?"
  - Space: "What would happen if you fell into Jupiter?"
  - Food: "Why does honey never go off?"
- Good for: facts, science, surprising answers (script 06), what if (script 04).
- Avoid: questions with a yes/no answer the viewer can guess.

## 02 Absurd number

**Status:** ready

Open with a number so big or so small it sounds wrong.

- Write it: the number first, said in full, then the thing it measures. Round it so it's easy to hear.
- Examples:
  - Nature: "Twenty quadrillion ants are alive right now."
  - Money: "Someone once paid ten thousand bitcoin for two pizzas."
  - Body: "Your blood vessels would wrap around the Earth twice."
- Good for: facts, space, money, did you know (script 09).
- Avoid: numbers that need a unit explained before they impress.

## 03 You're doing it wrong

**Status:** ready

Tell the viewer that something they do every day is wrong.

- Write it: name the everyday action precisely, then say it's wrong. The body shows the right way.
- Examples:
  - Food: "You've been cutting avocados wrong your whole life."
  - Tech: "You're charging your phone wrong."
  - Health: "You're brushing your teeth at the wrong time."
- Good for: life hacks, how it works (script 07), myth or fact (script 03).
- Avoid: when there isn't a clearly better way; viewers will say so in the comments.

## 04 Impossible image

**Status:** ready

Open on a picture that looks like it can't be real, and say what it is.

- Write it: describe what's on screen in the fewest words, as if it were normal. The visual does the work; the line confirms it.
- Examples:
  - Nature: "This lake is pink, and nobody added anything to it."
  - Engineering: "This bridge is held up by nothing but its own shape."
  - Animals: "This frog has a see-through belly. You can watch its heart beat."
- Good for: nature, space, engineering, any style that can draw the image well.
- Avoid: when the style can't show the image clearly in the first frame.

## 05 Unfinished sentence

**Status:** ready

Start a sentence and finish it only at the end of the video.

- Write it: the start of a sentence that needs its end ("The reason cats knock things off tables is..."). The ending beat completes it, which pairs naturally with ending 01 seamless loop.
- Examples:
  - Animals: "The real reason cats knock things off tables is..."
  - History: "The Eiffel Tower was almost torn down because..."
  - Psychology: "The first thing people notice about you is..."
- Good for: stories, loops, short videos (15–30 s).
- Avoid: long videos; the viewer forgets the open sentence.

## 06 Bold claim

**Status:** ready

Open with a strong statement, then prove it.

- Write it: one flat, confident sentence with no hedging. The body is the proof, with sources in `sources`.
- Examples:
  - History: "Napoleon wasn't short."
  - Science: "Glass is not a slow liquid."
  - Animals: "Goldfish have better memories than you think."
- Good for: myths, history, myth or fact (script 03).
- Avoid: claims you can't source; fact-check before the script is approved.

## 07 Countdown tease

**Status:** ready

Promise that the last item is the best one.

- Write it: say how many items there are and that the last one is the strongest. Then count down (script 02).
- Examples:
  - Animals: "Five animals that cheat death, and number one is immortal."
  - Tech: "Four gadgets you didn't know you needed. Wait for the last one."
  - Travel: "Three places you can't visit, and the last one is in Europe."
- Good for: lists, top N countdown (script 02).
- Avoid: when the last item isn't really the best; viewers notice.

## 08 POV

**Status:** ready

Put the viewer inside the situation: "POV: you are...".

- Write it: "POV:" then a specific person in a specific moment. Keep the rest of the video in the second person.
- Examples:
  - History: "POV: you built the pyramids and got paid in beer."
  - Jobs: "POV: you're the first person to taste coffee."
  - Animals: "POV: you're a sloth and you need the bathroom."
- Good for: stories, history, humour, mini story (script 10).
- Avoid: facts with no human moment in them.

## 09 Nobody talks about this

**Status:** ready

Frame the topic as overlooked or hidden.

- Write it: name the topic and claim it's ignored, then give the one detail that proves it's worth knowing.
- Examples:
  - Business: "Nobody talks about why every Apple ad shows nine forty-one."
  - History: "Nobody talks about the war that lasted thirty-eight minutes."
  - Health: "Nobody talks about what sitting all day does to your hips."
- Good for: facts, history, business, how it works.
- Avoid: overusing it on one page; it stops working after a few videos.

## 10 Before/after flash

**Status:** ready

Flash the finished result first, then go back to the start.

- Write it: one line over the finished result ("This is a pile of sand."), then rewind. The body builds it step by step, so it pairs with script 01 build it.
- Examples:
  - Engineering: "This started as a pile of sand." (over a glass skyscraper)
  - Food: "This is what one coffee bean becomes."
  - History: "This was a swamp two hundred years ago." (over a city)
- Good for: build it (script 01), transformations.
- Avoid: when the scenes can't show the finished result in the first frame.

## 11 Insider secret

**Status:** ready

Share what people in the field know and others don't.

- Write it: name the insiders ("pilots", "chefs", "museum guards"), then the secret, in one breath.
- Examples:
  - Jobs: "The pilot and co-pilot never eat the same meal."
  - Food: "Chefs salt pasta water far more than you'd dare."
  - Retail: "Supermarkets put milk at the back for a reason."
- Good for: jobs, business, how it works (script 07).
- Avoid: secrets that turn out to be common knowledge; check the comments of similar videos first.

## 12 Challenge

**Status:** ready

Dare the viewer to guess, spot or try something.

- Write it: a direct dare with a time limit or a condition ("in three seconds", "without pausing"). Give the answer late in the video.
- Examples:
  - Puzzles: "You have three seconds to spot the odd one out."
  - Language: "Bet you can't say this word without laughing."
  - Science: "Guess which weighs more. Most people get it wrong."
- Good for: quizzes, puzzles, X vs Y (script 08), myth or fact (script 03).
- Avoid: challenges the viewer can't take while scrolling.

## 13 Start at the climax

**Status:** ready

Open on the most dramatic moment, then rewind.

- Write it: the peak moment in the present tense, then "Here's how we got here" or a cut back. The body tells the story in order (script 05).
- Examples:
  - History: "The ship is sinking, and the band keeps playing."
  - Space: "Apollo 13 is two hundred thousand miles from home, and a tank just exploded."
  - Business: "The company has one day of money left."
- Good for: stories, history, story in 60 seconds (script 05).
- Avoid: stories whose climax needs set-up to make sense.

## 14 Relatable pain

**Status:** ready

Name a small frustration everyone has felt.

- Write it: describe the moment exactly, in the second person, so the viewer thinks "that's me". Then explain or fix it.
- Examples:
  - Tech: "Your phone dies at twenty percent. Here's why."
  - Home: "You walk into a room and forget why."
  - Food: "The ketchup won't come out, then it all comes out."
- Good for: life hacks, humour, how it works.
- Avoid: pains only a small group has.

## 15 Contrarian take

**Status:** ready

Disagree with something everyone believes.

- Write it: state the common belief, then reject it in a short second sentence. The body argues the case fairly.
- Examples:
  - Health: "Eight glasses of water a day? You don't need them."
  - History: "Vikings never wore horned helmets."
  - Productivity: "Waking up at five won't make you successful."
- Good for: myths, opinions, science, myth or fact (script 03).
- Avoid: contrarian takes on safety or health that you can't source; the reviewer agent flags them.
