# 05 Before and after

## What it's for

Tell a case study: the problem someone had, what was done, and the result in numbers, with their own words as proof. It shows that you deliver, which is stronger than saying so. It also covers plain before-and-after transformations.

## Slides

`cover → point (the problem) → compare → steps (what was done) → stat (the result) → quote → cta`

- `cover`: the result, or the person and the problem, in one line.
- `point`: the situation before, in plain words.
- `compare`: before against after, at most four lines per side, with `✗` for before and `✓` for after.
- `steps`: what was done, three to five steps.
- `stat`: the headline number of the result, with one line under it.
- `quote`: the customer's own words about it.
- `cta`: an invite for the reader who has the same problem.

For a short post drop the `steps` or the `quote`. Use an `image` slide for a real before-and-after picture.

## Cover

Lead with the result when it is strong, or with the starting problem when it is relatable, in 12 words or fewer, with the number or the problem as the accent.

- "How a bakery got *40% more* walk-ins"
- "From an empty window to a *queue* at the door"
- "Before and after: one shop, *one poster*"

## Writing rules

- Use a real case, with the customer's permission. Never invent results; never show a customer's name or logo without it.
- Give a number with its unit and its period ("in 8 weeks"), or leave the number out.
- The before and after lines in `compare` match one to one: the same thing, then and now.
- Say what you did, not what you are "passionate about".
- A quote is the customer's words, shortened but not changed. It goes with their first name or role.
- Do not promise the reader the same result; invite them to ask.

## Example

```markdown
---
title: A bakery window, before and after
post_type: 5
label: CASE STUDY
---

## cover
# How a bakery got *40% more* walk-ins
One window, one new poster, eight weeks.

## point
# The *problem*
Casa Aurora made great bread but sat on a quiet side street. People walked past without looking in, and the old window had faded notices from years before.

## compare
# Window, *then and now*
- ✗ Faded paper notices
- ✗ No prices on show
- ✗ Nothing to see from far away
- ✓ One large poster
- ✓ Prices in clear type
- ✓ A bold headline from the street

## steps
# What we *did*
- Visit | we
- Design | we
- Proof | owner
- Print and fit | we

## stat
# +40%
shop visits counted over the eight weeks after

## quote
# "People started asking about the poster before they asked about the bread."
Marta, owner of Casa Aurora

## cta
# A quiet window? *Let's talk.*
[Message @studionord]
```
